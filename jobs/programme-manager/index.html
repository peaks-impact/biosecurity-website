About the Zurich Biosecurity Hub

Advances in biotechnology and AI have changed the risk profile of pandemics. A future outbreak may not begin in nature, and the work of preventing that is short of people rather than of ideas. Switzerland is unusually well placed to contribute: ETH Zurich, the University of Zurich and EPFL are among Europe's strongest research environments in biomedicine and machine learning, Basel is Europe's largest life sciences cluster, and Geneva is where much of biological weapons governance takes place.

The Zurich Biosecurity Hub was set up in September 2026 to make it easier for people to enter this field and to build a career around reducing these risks. It grew out of a biosecurity paper reading group that has met in Zurich for about a year, and it now runs:

  • Biosecurity Futures, a public lecture series at ETH Zurich (five sessions in the autumn semester 2026)
  • A fellowship on catastrophic biological risk, adapted from the BlueDot Impact biosecurity curriculum, running in several cohorts per year
  • The paper reading group, in person in Zurich and online
  • One-to-one career advising
  • Coworking at PEAKS, an open offer for people working on biosecurity to work alongside Zurich's effective altruism and AI safety community

Key responsibilities

Programme delivery, end to end. Plan, promote, run and evaluate each programme in the calendar: the Biosecurity Futures sessions at ETH (speakers, rooms, registration, follow-up), the fellowship cohorts (call for applications, admissions, facilitator coordination, weekly sessions online and socials in Zurich), the paper reading group in cooperation with its volunteer leads, and the retreats and local meetups.

Flagship events 2027. Take operational responsibility for a conference on global catastrophic biological risk at ETH Zurich and for up to two hackathons (topics not fixed yet, but could be on clean air and personal protective equipment): venue, partners, participants, budget, logistics, on the day and afterwards.

Outreach and communications. Take overall responsibility for the Hub's outreach and make sure it is handled well. A contractor supports on communications, but the responsibility for getting it right stays with you. Grow the participant pipeline, with a focus on students, researchers and founders in life sciences, medicine, machine learning, engineering and policy.

Participant relationships and follow-up. Be the first point of contact for people who get in touch. Know who has come to what, notice who is showing momentum, and hand people on to career advising with the Director at the right moment. Keep in touch with fellowship alumni.

Measurement and reporting. Track registrations, attendance, cohort completions, advising requests and where people go afterwards. Collect structured feedback after each programme. Turn this into evidence the team can act on and into the reporting our funder needs.

Operations. Handle room bookings, budget tracking with the EACH back office, tools (Luma, Google Workspace, Airtable), contracts with paid facilitators, and the small everyday tasks that make a programme run on time.
